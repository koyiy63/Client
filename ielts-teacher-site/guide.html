Step-by-step: Become an IELTS teacher

Keep it simple. Build core knowledge, practice teaching with templates, then start tutoring. You can do this in 2–6 weeks depending on your time.

1) Understand how IELTS is scored

  • Read the official public band descriptors for Writing and Speaking.
  • Summarize in your own words: what Band 6 vs 7 vs 8 looks like.
  • Memorize 3–4 key differences for each band jump.

Writing band focus

Task Achievement, Coherence & Cohesion, Lexical Resource, Grammatical Range & Accuracy.

Speaking band focus

Fluency & Coherence, Lexical Resource, Grammatical Range & Accuracy, Pronunciation.

2) Practice with plug‑and‑play lesson plans

Run these exactly at first. Adjust later.

Listening (60–75 min)

  • 5 min: Warm-up vocab from the topic
  • 10 min: Predict answers from question types
  • 25 min: Play audio once; students answer
  • 10 min: Check + highlight synonyms/paraphrase
  • 10 min: Replay weak parts; focus on numbers/spelling

Reading (60–75 min)

  • 10 min: Skim titles, bold words, first lines
  • 20 min: Do one passage with time limit
  • 15 min: Review T/F/NG logic and matching
  • 15 min: Vocabulary building from text

Writing Task 1 (60 min)

  • 10 min: Identify chart type + 2–3 key trends
  • 15 min: Plan overview (highest/lowest, changes)
  • 25 min: Write with a 4‑paragraph template
  • 10 min: Feedback using band checklist

Writing Task 2 (75–90 min)

  • 10 min: Analyze question (type + keywords)
  • 15 min: Plan ideas (2 main ideas + examples)
  • 40 min: Write with 5‑paragraph template
  • 15 min: Feedback and upgrade sentences

Speaking (45–60 min)

  • 10 min: Part 1 fast Q&A with follow‑ups
  • 15 min: Part 2 card prep (mind map + linking)
  • 15 min: Part 3 opinion + reasons + examples
  • 10 min: Pronunciation: word stress + linking

3) Give clear feedback with checklists

  • Always say: What is good, what to fix, how to fix, next action.
  • Use a simple rubric: 0–5 for each criterion (adapted from bands).
  • Record 30‑sec voice notes to model pronunciation or intonation.

4) Start tutoring simply

  • Offer a free 15‑min consultation and a paid 60‑min trial lesson.
  • Set a basic package: 8 lessons (2 per skill) + 2 mock tests.
  • Price fairly for your market; focus on results and testimonials.

5) For Asian teachers: clarity over perfection

  • Accent is fine. Aim for clear sounds, word stress, and rhythm.
  • Be aware of L1 transfer: articles, plurals, prepositions, and verb tense consistency.
  • Model short, natural chunks: "I tend to…", "One key reason is…", "Compared with…"

6) Weekly learning plan for you (2–6 weeks)

  • Week 1: Read band descriptors; watch 2 model lessons per skill.
  • Week 2: Teach 2 practice lessons to friends; collect feedback.
  • Week 3: Create your own slides using the templates; run 2 paid trials.
  • Week 4+: Improve your feedback speed; add mock tests and homework kits.
