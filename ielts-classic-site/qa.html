Q&A Bank — Speaking follow‑ups (50+)

Answer formula: opinion → reason → example → wrap‑up. Open to reveal Band 6 and Band 7 samples.

Education (7)

Should homework be limited?
6: It should be balanced because too much causes stress; for example, students sleep late. So a moderate amount is better.
7: Limits make sense when tasks repeat classwork without feedback. Shorter, goal‑based homework improves learning while preserving rest.
Are exams the best way to assess?
6: Not always, because some students get nervous. Projects can show skills.
7: Exams test recall under time pressure; coursework measures research and collaboration. A mix is fairer overall.
Should university be free?
6: It helps poor families, but budgets are limited.
7: Free tuition widens access, yet sustainable funding needs caps or targeted aid to protect quality.
Is online learning as effective as in-person?
6: Sometimes; it saves time, but focus is hard.
7: It works when classes use interaction, deadlines, and feedback; otherwise engagement drops.
Should schools teach financial literacy?
6: Yes, it’s useful for life.
7: Budgeting and basic investing prevent debt; one module per year could change outcomes.
Do grades motivate students?
6: For some, yes; others feel pressure.
7: External rewards help short term; long‑term motivation needs feedback and autonomy.
Should schools ban phones?
6: Maybe in class, to avoid distraction.
7: Limit use during lessons, but allow supervised research and emergencies.

Technology (7)

Does social media improve communication?
6: Sometimes yes, it’s fast.
7: It increases reach but often reduces depth; scheduled calls improve understanding.
Should companies allow remote work?
6: Yes, it saves time.
7: Productivity rises with clear goals, outcome tracking, and regular team check‑ins.
Are smartphones harming focus?
6: A bit, because of notifications.
7: Attention suffers without boundaries; batch messages and use focus modes.
Is AI good for jobs?
6: Mixed; it replaces some roles.
7: It shifts tasks; workers who learn AI tools often become more productive and valuable.
Should children learn coding?
6: It can be helpful.
7: Logic and problem‑solving transfer across subjects; even simple block coding builds skills.
Do gadgets make life too convenient?
6: Maybe; people move less.
7: Convenience is fine if we design routines for exercise and real conversation.
Should screen time be limited at work?
6: Yes, take breaks.
7: Short breaks every hour protect eyes and improve creativity when people return.

Work & Economy (7)

Is job security more important than salary?
6: For many, yes; safety matters.
7: Depends on stage of life; parents often value stability for planning more than extra pay.
Should governments support small businesses?
6: Yes, they create jobs.
7: Grants and training boost local growth, but support should reward innovation and transparency.
Is lifelong learning necessary?
6: Yes, tech changes fast.
7: Skills expire quickly; micro‑courses keep workers adaptable.
Should internships be paid?
6: If possible, yes.
7: Paid roles widen access; otherwise only rich students benefit.
Are open offices effective?
6: Not for everyone.
7: They help quick chats but hurt deep work; mixed spaces work best.
Do uniforms improve professionalism?
6: They look tidy.
7: Uniforms reduce status signals; clear dress codes can have the same effect with more comfort.
Is entrepreneurship for everyone?
6: Not really; it’s risky.
7: It suits people who enjoy uncertainty and sales; others thrive in stable roles.

Health & Lifestyle (7)

How can cities promote exercise?
6: Build parks and bike lanes.
7: Safe routes + lighting and community events raise participation.
Do people eat healthier now?
6: Not really; fast food is common.
7: Awareness is higher, but time pressure still drives processed choices.
Is mental health discussed openly?
6: More than before.
7: Stigma is falling, but access is uneven; workplaces need training and referral paths.
Should schools teach cooking?
6: Yes, it’s practical.
7: Basic cooking reduces reliance on fast food and improves health long‑term.
Are sports essential for children?
6: Good for health.
7: Team games build discipline and social skills beyond fitness.
Should sugary drinks be taxed?
6: Maybe, to reduce use.
7: Taxes can cut consumption if combined with education and clean water access.
Does sleep affect productivity?
6: Yes, a lot.
7: Consistent 7–8 hours improves memory and mood; naps help shift workers.

Environment (7)

Should plastic bags be banned?
6: Yes, they pollute.
7: Bans work when alternatives are cheap and convenient.
Is climate change the biggest problem?
6: It’s very serious.
7: It multiplies other risks; adaptation and emissions cuts must happen together.
Should cities plant more trees?
6: Yes, for shade.
7: Urban trees lower heat, absorb noise, and improve mental health.
Are electric cars the solution?
6: They help.
7: They cut tailpipe emissions, but energy sources and mining matter too.
Should flights be limited?
6: Maybe long flights.
7: Incentives for trains and carbon pricing can shift behaviour fairly.
Is recycling effective?
6: Sometimes; people forget.
7: Clear labels and deposit systems raise rates significantly.
Should companies report emissions?
6: Yes, be honest.
7: Standard reporting lets investors compare and pressure improvements.

Society & Culture (7)

Is volunteering important?
6: It helps people.
7: It builds skills and networks; short, regular roles sustain motivation.
Should museums be free?
6: If possible, yes.
7: Free entry boosts access; rotating paid exhibits can fund operations.
Is celebrity influence positive?
6: Sometimes, for charity.
7: It raises awareness but can oversimplify issues; partner with experts.
Do traditions matter today?
6: Yes, for identity.
7: Traditions connect generations; adapt forms to include younger people.
Should public art be funded?
6: Yes, it’s beautiful.
7: Art improves shared spaces and tourism; small grants have big impact.
Is censorship ever justified?
6: For safety, maybe.
7: Clear, narrow rules (e.g., incitement) protect speech while limiting harm.
Should libraries prioritise digital media?
6: Many people like e‑books.
7: Hybrid services reach more users; keep quiet study spaces too.

Travel & Tourism (6)

Is budget travel educational?
6: Yes, you learn a lot.
7: Tight budgets push travellers to interact locally and solve problems.
Should tourist numbers be limited?
6: In crowded places, yes.
7: Caps plus timed tickets protect heritage without killing income.
Do travel influencers help destinations?
6: They promote places.
7: They can spread crowds; partnerships should promote off‑peak visits.
Is solo travel safe?
6: It depends.
7: With research and local sim cards, risk drops; share live locations with friends.
Should cities tax short‑term rentals?
6: Maybe to help housing.
7: Taxes and limits can balance tourism and local supply.
Are package tours still popular?
6: Older people like them.
7: They reduce planning stress; younger travellers prefer flexible options.

Family & Relationships (6)

Should schools teach parenting basics?
6: Useful for teens.
7: Basic child development reduces future stress for new parents.
Is it better to live with parents after graduation?
6: It saves money.
7: Short term it helps savings; set timelines and share bills to stay fair.
Do busy jobs harm relationships?
6: Sometimes; no time.
7: Schedules help; weekly routines protect time together.
Should elderly people work part‑time?
6: If they want.
7: Light roles keep skills active and reduce loneliness.
Is marriage less important today?
6: Some think so.
7: Commitment takes many forms; legal benefits still matter for families.
Should schools teach conflict resolution?
6: Yes, for teamwork.
7: Role‑play reduces bullying and builds negotiation skills.
