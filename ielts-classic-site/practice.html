Open each task to reveal model answers and brief explanations.

Reading — T/F/NG

Passage (short): Many cities are adding bike lanes to reduce traffic and improve public health. Studies show that when routes are safe, more people cycle regularly.

1. Bike lanes are built only to improve health. (T/F/NG)
Answer: F — The text mentions reducing traffic and improving health; not only health.
2. People cycle more when they feel safe. (T/F/NG)
Answer: T — "when routes are safe, more people cycle".
3. Studies disagree about bike lanes. (T/F/NG)
Answer: NG — No statement about disagreement.

Listening (script-based form fill)

Script (read aloud): Hello, I'd like to book a tour on June 14 for two adults. My email is maya.khan@example.com.

Form answers
Date: June 14; People: two adults; Email: maya.khan@example.com

Trick: Predict word type (date, number, email) before listening.

Writing Task 1 (overview focus)

Prompt: The chart shows yearly sales from 2018 to 2022.

Model answer (concise)
Overall, sales increased steadily from 2018 to 2022, with a modest dip in 2020. In 2018, figures were lowest; they rose in 2019, fell slightly in 2020, then climbed to a peak by 2022.

Trick: Give 2–3 big trends; avoid minor fluctuations.

Writing Task 2 (opinion)

Prompt: Some think remote work increases productivity. Do you agree?

Model answer (Band 6)
I agree because people save time and feel comfortable at home. For example, without commuting, workers have more time to rest and focus. Overall, it usually helps.
Model answer (Band 7)
I generally agree. Productivity often rises due to flexible schedules and fewer commutes. For instance, teams that set clear goals can use quiet hours for deep work. However, it depends on strong communication and measurable outcomes.

Speaking — Part 2

Card: Describe a time you helped someone solve a problem.

Model (Band 6)
I helped my classmate with a project. First, we listed the issues. Then I showed a simple method I knew. Finally, we tested it and it worked. I felt happy because it was practical.
Model (Band 7)
Last semester I helped a friend fix a data issue. First, we defined the problem and narrowed it to a formatting error. Then we created a small script to clean the file. As a result, the analysis ran smoothly, and we saved a lot of time.

Trick: Tell 2–3 mini‑stories; link with first, then, finally.
